About the Author

Who He Is

David A. Robertson is a member of Norway House Cree Nation. He is an author, podcaster, public speaker, and social advocate who lives and works in Winnipeg, Manitoba. He writes mainly for children and youth, and his books bring Indigenous identity, culture, and history to younger readers.

What Led Him to Writing

Growing up as a Cree child in Winnipeg, Robertson felt disconnected from his heritage because his school did not teach Indigenous cultures or histories. He also found textbooks boring but was drawn to comics. He recognized that other children might also feel disconnected from their histories or find traditional textbooks unengaging. This led him to write graphic novels, a format that could both entertain young readers and teach them about Indigenous history and experiences.

His Writing Journey

1

Comics Opened the Door

His interest in comics showed him that stories could hold a young reader’s attention while also teaching history and culture.

2

7 Generations

In 2012, his four-part graphic novel saga brought wider attention to his work through one Indigenous family’s story across three centuries and seven generations.

3

Classrooms and Libraries

The series reached readers across Canada, where teachers and librarians used it to inspire young people and begin meaningful conversations.

4

A Growing Career

Robertson went on to write more than 25 books. Works such as When We Were Alone and The Misewa Saga bring Indigenous characters, histories, cultures, and experiences to young readers.
